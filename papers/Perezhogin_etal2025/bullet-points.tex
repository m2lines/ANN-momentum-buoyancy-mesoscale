\begin{figure}[h!]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{offline-4.pdf}%{generalization-2.pdf}
\caption{(a) Schematic of the Artificial neural network (ANN) parameterizations (Eqs. \eqref{eq:model_1} or \eqref{eq:model_3}) working on $3\times3$ horizontal stencil.
(b), (c), (d)
Role of the dimensional scaling in ANN (Eq. \eqref{eq:model_3}, center column) compared to baseline ANN (Eq. \eqref{eq:model_1}, right column) for generalization across depth and resolutions. (b) Predictions of the subfilter fluxes at the depth ($5\mathrm{m}$) and resolution ($0.9^\circ$) used for training (testing data is separated by 10 years), (c) prediction at the same depth but the unseen resolution ($0.4^\circ$). (d) R-squared metric for various depths and resolutions not seen during training, where panels (b) and (c) correspond to yellow and orange boxes, respectively.}
\label{fig:offline}
\end{figure}

Bullet points for Figure 1:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We study how the ANN model architecture affects its generalization ability
    \item We demonstrate that unit invariance is essential to generalize to unseen grid spacings and depths
    \item Parameterization without unit invariance has a reasonable correlation on unseen data, but the magnitude of prediction is wrong, and thus the R-squared is low
    \item Unit invariance helps to improve the prediction even on the data similar to that used for training
    \item ANN can be informed with grid spacing differently, by passing it as an input feature (Eq. \eqref{eq:model_2}). However, this would result in less efficient use of training data and less reliable predictions outside of the range of grid spacings used for training.
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[h!]
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{online-1.pdf}
\caption{Left column: effect of increasing resolution of ocean model from $1/4^\circ$ to  $1/10^\circ$ in CM2-O climate models \cite{griffies2015impacts}. Right column: effect of ANN parameterization in coarse ocean model OM4 \cite{adcroft2019gfdl} ($1/4^\circ$). (a) and (b): diagnosed and predicted by ANN upscale kinetic energy transfer (positive values represent backscatter), (c) and (d): difference (denoted as  $\Delta$) in Eddy Kinetic Energy (EKE), (e) and (f): difference in Available Potential Energy (APE), and (g) and (h): barotropic streamfunction of coarse unparameterized model in contours and its difference in color (zero B.C. is applied at tip of South America; contour interval is 20Sv, with positive, negative and zero values in solid, dashed and thick contours, respectively).}
\end{figure}

Bullet points for Figure 2:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The parameterization successfully predicts upscale energy transfer (backscatter) in unseen scenarios (different numerical model, different resolution, online evaluation)
    \item Upscale energy transfer is slightly overestimated, however, this is compensated by imposed dissipation in the ocean model (Smagorinsky)
    \item Contrary to upgradient backscatter, our parameterization drains energy near the lateral boundaries, as it is expected to stabilize the boundary currents
    \item The effect of parameterization should be similar to increasing horizontal resolution
    \item Similarly to idealized studies, we quantify the parameterization effect on kinetic energy, potential energy and horizontal circulation
    \item Parameterization increases the eddy kinetic energy by roughly the right amount, however, the EKE increase is confined to the western boundaries and there is missing variability in open Atlantic and Pacific oceans, and in Subpolar Gyres.
    \item EKE increase in Southern Ocean has a complicated spatial pattern related to the interaction between WBCs (Brazil, Aghulas current, East Australian Current) and ACC, and qualitatively reproduced with the parameterization.
    \item More energetic eddies increase the resolved mixing and drain APE from the Southern Ocean by slumping the isopycnals, in qualitative accordance with the high-resolution data.
    \item Drain of APE correlates with the weakening of the barotropic transport in the Sothern Ocean via thermal wind balance in high resolution and parameterized models.
    
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[h!]
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{online-2.pdf}
\caption{Comparison of ANN parameterization to negative viscosity backscatter parameterization \cite{chang2023remote, jansen2015energy}. Effect of mesoscale eddies is shown in its impact on KE, APE, and Drake Passage transport. Effect of restratification is demonstrated by a decrease of the globally-integrated potential temperature. Response of the AMOC and its connection to deep water formation (quantified by winter MLD) are shown. Change in the meridional heat flux and its impact on the ice thickness are shown. Response in the overturning in Southern Ocean demonstrates strengthening of overturning responsible for reducing slope of isopycnals.}
\end{figure}

Bullet points for Figure 3:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Response in some major climatological metrics, such as heat transport or AMOC, to resolving or parameterizing mesoscale eddies is not robust across different climate models  \cite{hewitt2020resolving}. The joint response of ACC and AMOC is even more complicated across different ocean models. Thus, here we compare the response with the response of another traditional backscatter parameterization.
    \item We demonstrate that the response due to ANN parameterization is remarkably similar to the response due to traditional backscatter parameterization and includes the following expected properties:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Increase of KE
        \item Reduction of APE
        \item Cooling of the ocean due to restratification by resolved eddies \cite{griffies2015impacts}
        \item Reduction of the Drake Passage transport due to weakening the baroclinic component of the flow
        \item Strengthening the DWBC as it is seen in deep branch of AMOC
        \item Enhanced poleward heat transport in Southern Ocean related to APE release
        \item Melting (formation) of the sea ice in Southern (Northern) hemispheres as a result of heat tranport response.
        \item Enhanced counterclockwise residual circulation in the Southern Ocean related to APE release.
    \end{itemize}
    \item The main difference is observed in the response in Atlantic ocean which is related to difference in deep water formation.
    \item As opposed to the Chang2023, our ANN parameterization shallows the mixed layer depth in Labrador Sea closer to the observations, resulting in suppressed deep water formation and weaker AMOC. 
    \item Another reason for weaker upper AMOC branch may be enhanced dissipation of boundary currents by the ANN, as compared to the traditional backscatter which enhances the boundary currents. 
    
\end{itemize}
