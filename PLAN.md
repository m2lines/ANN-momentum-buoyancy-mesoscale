# Part 2 — Buoyancy flux parameterization with realistic grids

Working plan for extending the data-driven mesoscale eddy-induced transport parameterization from idealized 2-layer configurations (Part 1, Balwada et al. 2026) to realistic 3D stratification using CM2.6 training data. The main change from Part 1 is that the target quantity shifts from thickness fluxes to ρ-fluxes, which are the natural 3D analog and directly relevant for ocean circulation. The training pipeline will reuse much of Pavel Perezhogin's pipeline from Perezhogin et al. 2025.

## Goal

**Part 2** of the Balwada et al. two-paper series on ANN-based parameterizations of mesoscale eddy-induced transport. Part 1 establishes the method in 2-layer Phillips and Double-Gyre configurations. Part 2 tests whether the same parameterization structure generalizes to realistic 3D stratification when trained on CM2.6 data.

- **Target quantity**: subfilter ρ-fluxes (Fx, Fy) — 3D/arbitrary vertical grid analog of Part 1's thickness fluxes
- **Training data**: CM2.6 at coarsening factors 4, 9, 12, 15 (reuse Pavel's pipeline)
- **Online platform**: MOM6, via the `rho_flux_ANN_gfdl_ready` branch 
- **Co-authors**: TBD; Balwada lead, Perezhogin co-dev

Their reported R² (Fig 1d, `'R2'`/`'R2_away'`) is this **divergence/forcing** skill (~0.79 at 0.9°/5m). **Buoyancy (us):** our scheme does **NOT** apply ∇·F_h. We feed the predicted horizontal flux into the Ferrari (2010) **streamfunction** Υ=F_h/|∇₃ρ|, and the forcing is the divergence of the eddy-induced *advective transport* derived from Υ — not the flux divergence. **So the quantity we actually need the network to get right is the flux (which feeds Υ), or ideally Υ / the eddy transport itself — NOT ∇·F_h.** Implications: (1) our headline = flux R² is the right offline metric (the flux is the network's product and Υ's input); (2) training a buoyancy model on ∇·F_h (Perezhogin-style) optimizes a quantity our scheme doesn't use, so it is an informative *comparison* but probably **not** the right objective for buoyancy — a **streamfunction-/transport-targeted loss is the principled future direction**; (3) a head-to-head R² with Perezhogin (his divergence vs our flux) is inherently not apples-to-apples. **→ belongs in the paper Discussion.** Experiments launched 2026-06-21: (a) **momentum reproduction** (`slurm_repro_momentum.sh`, `train_script.py` forcing loss, single-point 0.9°/5m, hidden [20], scaling, symmetries) — does THIS pipeline reproduce their ~0.79 forcing R²? validates we use their code right vs buoyancy being intrinsically harder; (b) **divergence-loss buoyancy model** (ρ analog: predict ρ flux, loss on ∇·F) — comparison point, with the streamfunction caveat above noted.
